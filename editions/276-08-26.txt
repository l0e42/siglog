NEW CHAIR OF SIGLOG: ANUJ DAWAR
ANNOUNCEMENT -s SIGLOG MATTER
* I am writing to you as the new chair of SIGLog with news of changes happening at the organization.  I have recently taken over from Catuscia Palamidessi in this position.  At the same time, Prakash Panangaden has taken over the position of vice-chair.  Sandra Alves and Elaine Pimentel continue in their roles as Secretary and Treasurer respectively.  I wanted to start by thanking Catuscia for her dedicated service to the organization through a difficult time.
  Secondly, I want to provide you with an update on SIGLog and its activities. As many of you know, SIGLog has had some financial difficulties in recent years caused by ACM requiring an increased contribution from SIGs during its transition to open-access publishing. A year ago, we were looking at the real prospect of dissolving SIGLog or seeing it merge with another SIG. I am happy to say that these issues are now resolved. We have reached a settlement with the ACM which sees our contribution restored to its previous level, balanced by our income from the ACM Digital Library. This leaves us with income from membership fees and conferences to support the important activities that SIGLog funds. These include the Women in Logic (WiL) meetings, Logic Mentoring workshops, the Church award and student support. We are now able to look forward to a period of rebuilding and taking new initiatives. I am keen on hearing from the SIGLog membership, and the wider Logic in Computer Science community on how SIGLog can serve you. I would be very happy to receive any suggestions that you might have.
  This past week I have been attending FLOC in Lisbon which is a quadrennial gathering of our community, and one of the main purposes of SIGLog. This year it sees over 2000 participants taking part in a very lively event. It is a reminder that we have a vibrant research community and the vital role that SIGLog plays in representing this community within ACM and the broader field of computer science.
  In closing, I would like to urge you all to renew your SIGLog membership or take one out if you do not already have one to support this important work. Here's the link: https://siglog.org
 - Anuj Dawar. anuj.dawar@cl.cam.ac.uk

* AIMS AND SCOPE
  LPNMR 2026 is the eighteenth in the series of international conferences on logic programming and non-monotonic reasoning. LPNMR is a forum for exchanging ideas on declarative logic programming, non-monotonic reasoning, and knowledge representation. The aim of the conference is to facilitate interactions between researchers and practitioners interested in the design and implementation of logic-based programming languages and database systems, and those working in knowledge representation and non-monotonic reasoning. LPNMR strives to encompass theoretical and experimental studies that have led or will lead to advances in declarative programming and knowledge representation, as well as their use in practical applications.
  LPNMR 2026 aims to bring together researchers from LPNMR and neighboring areas in order to share research experiences, promote collaboration and identify directions for joint future research.
* VENUE
  LPNMR 2026 will be held on the campus of the University of Klagenfurt in September 2026. Klagenfurt is the capital of the state Carinthia in the south of Austria. The name Klagenfurt was first mentioned in the late 12th century and legend has it that Klagenfurt was founded after a couple of brave men had slain the abominable Lindwurm, a winged dragon. The city is located next to the lake Woerthersee, one of the most beautiful lakes in Austria, surrounded by several forest-covered hills and mountains. Being a small city, with a Renaissance-style city center reflecting 800 years of history and with an Italian influence, Klagenfurt is a pleasant place to live and work.
